The **public domain** is made up of creative works that are not protected by copyright or similar exclusive rights. Because no one owns them, anyone may copy, adapt, perform, publish or sell them without permission.

## How works enter the public domain

A work can enter the public domain in several ways:

1. **Expiration**: copyright lasts for a limited time. In many countries it ends a set number of years after the author's death, often 70 years.
2. **Ineligibility**: some things cannot be copyrighted at all, such as plain facts, ideas, and in many places short phrases or works with no creative choices.
3. **Dedication**: an author can give up their rights on purpose, for example with the [[cc0|CC0]] public domain dedication.
4. **Government works**: in some countries, works made by the national government are not copyrighted. In the United States this applies to works made by federal employees as part of their jobs.

## Why it matters

The public domain is a shared foundation for culture and science. Classic novels, old paintings and music, mathematical results and historical records can be republished, remixed and taught without cost. Projects like [[opendb|OpenDB]] add to it by publishing new work directly into the public domain.
