**Photosynthesis** is the process by which plants, algae and some bacteria capture the energy of light and store it in sugar. It produces the oxygen in Earth's air and is the starting point of almost every food chain.

## The overall reaction

In plants, photosynthesis can be summarized as:

`6 CO2 + 6 H2O + light energy → C6H12O6 + 6 O2`

Six molecules of carbon dioxide and six of water become one molecule of glucose and six molecules of oxygen.

## Where it happens

In plants and algae, photosynthesis takes place in **chloroplasts**, small structures inside cells. Chloroplasts contain **chlorophyll**, a green pigment that absorbs red and blue light strongly and reflects green light, which is why leaves look green.

## Two stages

1. **Light-dependent reactions**: in the thylakoid membranes, light energy splits water molecules. This releases oxygen and produces energy carriers called ATP and NADPH.
2. **The Calvin cycle**: in the stroma around the thylakoids, the cell uses ATP and NADPH to fix carbon dioxide from the air into sugars.

## Importance

Photosynthesis provides the oxygen that most living things breathe and the energy stored in food. Fossil fuels such as coal and oil are made from the remains of organisms that once captured energy this way. The water a plant takes in is part of the [[water-cycle|water cycle]], and some of it returns to the air through transpiration.
