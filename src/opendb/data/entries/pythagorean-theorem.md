The **Pythagorean theorem** is a basic fact of geometry about right triangles, the triangles with one 90° angle. It says that the area of the square on the longest side (the **hypotenuse**) equals the total area of the squares on the other two sides.

![Squares on the sides of a 3-4-5 triangle](media:pythagoras-diagram)

## Statement

If the two shorter sides have lengths *a* and *b*, and the hypotenuse has length *c*, then:

`a² + b² = c²`

For example, a triangle with sides 3 and 4 has a hypotenuse of 5, because 9 + 16 = 25.

## Pythagorean triples

Sets of three whole numbers that fit the theorem are called **Pythagorean triples**. Examples include (3, 4, 5), (5, 12, 13) and (8, 15, 17). Multiplying a triple by any whole number gives another one, such as (6, 8, 10).

## History and proofs

The theorem is named after the Greek philosopher Pythagoras, but the relationship was known much earlier: a Babylonian clay tablet known as Plimpton 322 lists Pythagorean triples. Hundreds of different proofs exist, including proofs that rearrange four copies of the triangle inside a large square.

## Uses

The theorem gives the straight-line distance between two points, so it is used in maps, navigation, construction, computer graphics and physics. It also connects to other number facts, such as which numbers are [[prime-number|prime]] (every prime that leaves remainder 1 when divided by 4 is a sum of two squares).
